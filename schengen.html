What is a Schengen visa?

The Schengen visa is a short-stay visa (Type C) for visits of up to 90 days in any 180-day period. It is issued by one Schengen country but lets you move freely through the whole Schengen area once you are inside.

There are 29 Schengen countries: Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland.

Who needs one?

It depends on your passport, not where you live. British passport holders do not need a Schengen visa for short tourism, business or family visits. If you hold a non-British passport, you may need one — even if you have lived in the UK for years.

We check your nationality, your UK immigration status and the length of your leave to remain before advising. The consulate will want to see that you can legally return to the UK after the trip.

The 90/180 rule

You can spend a maximum of 90 days in the Schengen area in any rolling 180-day period. The count includes every Schengen country, not just the one that issued the visa. Days spent in the UK, Ireland and non-Schengen countries do not count.

If you have travelled to the Schengen area recently, tell us the dates. We check the calculation before you apply, because a previous long stay can affect the new visa.

Which country do I apply to?

Apply to the country where you will spend the most nights. If two countries are equal, apply to the country you enter first. The country you fly into is not automatically the right one.

We confirm the main destination and the correct visa application centre before any government fee is paid.

What the application includes

  • A completed and signed application form
  • Passport and recent photographs
  • Travel insurance with at least €30,000 medical cover
  • Flight reservations and proof of accommodation
  • Bank statements and evidence of income
  • Employment, study or business evidence
  • Proof of UK immigration status, where applicable
  • A short cover letter explaining the trip

Our document checklist explains each item in more detail.

How long does it take?

Most applications are decided within 15 calendar days of the consulate receiving the file, and many are faster. Complex files can take up to 45 days. Appointment availability varies by country and season, so start four to six weeks before travel where you can.
