Which Schengen country should you apply to?

The main-destination rule explained, with examples for multi-country trips, transit and equal-length stays.

You cannot simply pick the Schengen country with the easiest appointment. The consulate that receives your application must have jurisdiction over your trip. Get this wrong and the file can be refused or returned without being assessed.

The main-destination rule

Apply to the country where you will spend the most nights. That is the main destination. The country you fly into is irrelevant if you move on the next day.

Example: you fly to Paris, spend two nights there, take the train to Amsterdam for four nights, then fly home from Amsterdam. Your main destination is the Netherlands, so you apply to the Dutch consulate, even though you entered through France.

When the nights are equal

If you are spending the same number of nights in two or more countries, apply to the country you enter first. Keep the bookings and travel tickets that show the order of travel.

Example: three nights in Portugal, three nights in Spain, entering through Lisbon. You apply to Portugal.

Day trips and transits

A day trip does not usually change the main destination, because you are not spending the night. Airport transit is different: some nationalities need an airport transit visa even if they never leave the terminal. Check this before booking a connecting flight.

Cruises and multi-country tours

For a cruise, the main destination is normally the port where the ship starts or ends, or the country with the most port calls, depending on the itinerary. Tour operators should provide a schedule you can submit. If the trip is packaged, ask the operator which consulate they expect you to use.

What if I am visiting family?

The same rule applies, but you will also need an invitation letter and evidence of the host's status. If you are staying with family in one country and touring others, count the nights, not the emotional centre of the trip.

Why the appointment is not the deciding factor

Appointment availability changes weekly. Choosing a consulate because it has a slot next Tuesday, when another country is clearly your main destination, creates a mismatch the decision-maker will notice. We check jurisdiction first, then build the timetable around the correct consulate.
