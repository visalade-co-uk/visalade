The Schengen visa documents that actually get checked

A practical checklist of the documents UK-based applicants should prepare for a Schengen visa, and the small mistakes that cause most delays.

Every Schengen consulate publishes its own document list, but the core file is remarkably consistent. What changes is the evidence the decision-maker wants to see for your particular trip. The aim is simple: prove who you are, why you are travelling, that you can afford it, and that you will come back.

1. Identity and status

Start with your passport. It should be valid for at least three months beyond your planned return date and issued within the last ten years. Include your UK residence permit, eVisa share code or legacy BRP if you are not a British citizen. If your name has changed, add the marriage certificate or deed poll.

2. The application form and photographs

The form must match your passport exactly, including middle names and the order of your names. Two recent colour photographs, taken to the current Schengen specification, are normally required. Do not reuse a photo from an old application.

3. Travel and accommodation

Flight reservations should show your name and the dates you have declared. Hotel bookings should cover every night of the trip. If you are staying with friends or family, include an invitation letter, proof of their address and a copy of their residence permit or ID. A gap in accommodation is one of the most common reasons a file is sent back.

4. Money

Most consulates ask for three to six months of bank statements. They are looking for regular income, a balance that is consistent with the trip you have planned, and no unexplained large deposits made just before applying. If a relative is funding you, add their statements and a signed sponsorship letter.

5. Employment, study or business

Employees should provide a recent letter from their employer confirming role, salary and approved leave. Self-employed applicants should add business registration, recent invoices and tax returns. Students need a letter from their school or university. Retirees can provide pension statements.

6. Insurance

Travel insurance must cover the whole Schengen area for the full trip and include at least €30,000 of medical emergency cover, including repatriation. A policy that starts a day late or covers only one country is a common and avoidable problem.

7. The cover letter

A short, factual cover letter ties the file together: who you are, where you are going, why, how you are paying, and when you are returning. It is not a place for long stories. One page, plain language, no contradictions with the rest of the evidence.

What we check before anything is submitted

We read the whole file as a decision-maker would. Dates, names, balances and bookings all have to agree. If something does not add up, we tell you before the appointment rather than after a refusal. That is the part most applicants cannot do for their own paperwork, because they know what they meant to write.
